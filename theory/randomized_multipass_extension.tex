We now bound what a charged compiler must spend on the streams of
\cref{def:w1-dispersed-stream} and show that the bounds are attained.  Put
\begin{equation}
 L_\delta(m,K):=(1-\delta)m\log_2K-h_2(\delta),
 \qquad 0\leq\delta<\tfrac12.
 \label{eq:w1-Ldelta}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[$K$-ary randomized multipass recoverability frontier]
\label{thm:w1-unified-frontier}
Let $Q\geq3$, $2\leq K\leq Q$, and
$0\leq\epsilon<\sin(\pi/(2Q))$.  Let a possibly randomized compiler be
correct within projective error $\epsilon$, with probability at least
$1-\delta$ for every $x\in[K]_0^m$ and every valid $r$-round dispersed
representation of $x$.  Assume that it makes at most $p=p(m)$ forward
passes in the charged model of \cref{def:w1-pass-accounting}.  Then:
\begin{gather}
 \overline B_{\mathrm{rel}}^{\mathrm{out}}\geq L_\delta(m,K),
 \qquad
 \overline B_{\mathrm{self}}^{\mathrm{out}}\geq L_\delta(m,K),
 \label{eq:w1-output-lower}\\
 \overline A_p+\overline\Sigma^{\to}_p
 \geq L_\delta(m,K)-\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil.
 \label{eq:w1-pass-lower}
\end{gather}
In particular, for the worst-input expected crossing cap $S$ of
\cref{def:w1-pass-accounting},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\overline A_p+pS\\
 &\quad\geq(1-\delta)m\log_2K-h_2(\delta)-\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil,
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:w1-frontier-box}
\end{equation}
and \emph{a fortiori} $\overline A_p+(2p-1)S$ obeys the same bound.
$S$ includes every field of \eqref{eq:info-budget}; it is not a RAM-only
bound.  For the tuned choice of \cref{cor:w1-epsilon-cyclic}, both right-hand sides
are $\Omega((1-\delta)m\log(1/\epsilon))$ for fixed
$\delta<1/2$ and $p=2^{o(m)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \eqref{eq:w1-output-lower}, take $X$ uniform on $[K]_0^m$ and fix one
valid dispersed representation for each value of $X$.
The packing separation is greater than $2\epsilon$, so nearest-packing
decoding recovers $X$ from a successful output.  Fano's inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
 I(X;C)\geq m\log_2K-h_2(\delta)
 -\delta\log_2(K^m-1)\geq L_\delta(m,K).
\end{equation*}
Expected length in either separately fixed prefix-free output code is at
least this mutual information; a worst-input expectation is at least the
uniform average.

For \eqref{eq:w1-pass-lower}, independently choose a uniform prefix
aggregate $U\in\mathbb Z_Q^m$, put it in the first prefix round, put zero
in any other prefix rounds, and set the last share $V=X-U\pmod Q$.
Then $V$ is uniform and independent of $X$, so
$H(X\mid V)=m\log_2K$.  Run the compiler and record the forward
transcript
\begin{equation*}
 T:=\bigl(c,\ \mathsf s_1,O_1,\ \ldots,\ \mathsf s_c,O_c,\ O_{\mathrm{fin}}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where $c\in\{0,\ldots,p\}$ is the number of forward crossings, written in
$\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil$ bits; $\mathsf s_t$ is the five-field snapshot at the
$t$-th crossing $A\to B$; $O_t$ is the output written while scanning $A$
just before that crossing; and $O_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is any output written in
$A$ after the last crossing (after a final rewind, or in a run that never
reaches $B$).  Given $T$ and $V$, Bob reproduces the whole run.
From $\mathsf s_t$ and $V$ he simulates the scan of $B$, including the random
tape and dynamic control that $\mathsf s_t$ serializes, and thereby obtains the
Bob-side output and the rewind snapshot; the rewind snapshot is therefore a
function of $(\mathsf s_t,V)$ and need not be transmitted.  The control field
of $\mathsf s_t$ records the emitted-prefix length, which gives $|O_t|$ once
$\mathsf s_t$ is read, so $\mathsf s_t$ is placed before $O_t$.  After the $c$-th
crossing, $O_{\mathrm{fin}}$ completes a codeword of the prefix-free output
code and is therefore self-delimiting.  Hence $T$ is a prefix-free code for
each fixed $V$.  It determines the final output, the output decoder recovers
$X$ with error at most $\delta$, and conditional Fano yields
\begin{equation*}
 I(X;T\mid V)\geq L_\delta(m,K).
\end{equation*}
The expected length of a prefix-free code is at least this conditional
information, and that length is
$\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil+A_p+\Sigma^{\to}_p$.  Maximizing over inputs proves
\eqref{eq:w1-pass-lower}.  Since each of the at most $p$ forward crossings
has expected maximum budget at most $S$, the boxed form follows.  The tuned
consequence follows from \cref{cor:w1-epsilon-cyclic}.
\end{proof}

Only the $p$ forward crossings enter: of the $2p-1$ alternations that $p$
forward scans make, as in the standard simulation of $p$ passes by a
$(2p-1)$-message protocol~\cite{alon1999space,guha2008tight}, each rewind
snapshot is a function of the preceding forward snapshot and of the last
round.  A random-access input oracle, uncharged rereading outside the
counted passes, and reverse scans are different models.  Neither
\eqref{eq:w1-output-lower} nor \eqref{eq:w1-frontier-box} converts
self-contained bits into gates.  For one pass and zero error the bound
has an integer form, and a compact output forces the whole of it into the
state:

\begin{theorem}[One-pass zero-error bound]
\label{thm:w1-one-pass-mask}
\label{thm:main-tradeoff}
Let a deterministic one-pass compiler be correct to
$\epsilon<\sin(\pi/(2Q))$ on every valid stream of
\cref{def:w1-dispersed-stream} with $K\in\{Q-1,Q\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 A_1+B_{\mathrm{cut}}(c_{A\to B,1})
 \geq\left\lceil m\log_2Q\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:w1-one-pass-mask}
\end{equation}
If, moreover, the compiler writes its output in the family-relative code
$E_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and every final output satisfies
$B_{\mathrm{rel}}\leq m\log_2K+\kappa\log_2(m+2)$ for a constant
$\kappa$, then
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathrm{cut}}(c_{A\to B,1})
 \geq\left\lceil m\log_2Q\right\rceil-\kappa\log_2(m+2).
 \label{eq:compact-cut-floor}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $u\in\mathbb Z_Q^m$, take the canonical prefix with aggregate
$u$ ($u$ in the first round, zero in the other prefix rounds).  For
$u\neq u'$ choose one common final share $v$: $v=0$ if $K=Q$; if $K=Q-1$,
at each coordinate at most two values of $v_j$ make $u_j+v_j$ or
$u'_j+v_j$ equal to the excluded aggregate $Q-1$, and $Q\geq3$ leaves an
allowed one.  Both completions are valid and their aggregates differ
wherever $u$ and $u'$ differ, so by \cref{thm:w1-exact-separation} no
circuit is within $\epsilon$ of both targets.  The suffix computation is
a function of the snapshot and the suffix, and a run that halted inside
the prefix would give one output for two different targets, so every run
reaches the crossing and the map $u\mapsto\mathsf s_u\cdot O_A(u)$, the
snapshot followed by the committed prefix, is injective.  The snapshot is
self-delimiting and records the length of the committed prefix, so these
messages form a prefix-free set, and Kraft's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:w1-one-pass-mask}.  For \eqref{eq:compact-cut-floor}, fix any
valid prefix, with aggregate $u$.  The $K^m$ final shares $v$ with
$u+v\in[K]_0^m$ reach every aggregate, so they yield $K^m$ distinct
codewords of $E_{\mathrm{rel}}$, all extending the committed prefix and of
length at most $m\log_2K+\kappa\log_2(m+2)$.  Kraft's inequality applied
to their suffixes bounds every committed prefix by $\kappa\log_2(m+2)$,
and the injectivity argument above, applied to the canonical prefixes,
then bounds the snapshot.
\end{proof}

For $Q=3$ and binary aggregates, \cref{thm:w1-one-pass-mask} is the
two-round masked-share device executed in
\cref{sec:physical-dispersed,sec:commitment}: a deferred compiler with
compact output carries at least $\lceil m\log_23\rceil-O(\log m)$ bits
across the cut, whereas a compiler given the aggregate vector copies its
bits into aggregate mode, where base-2 blocks are exact, and so emits
$m+O(\log m)$ output bits with $O(\log m)$ state.
