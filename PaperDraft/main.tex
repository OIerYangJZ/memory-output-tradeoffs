\begin{abstract}
Time-dependent schedules split the angle of each rotation in a commuting
layer across many rounds, and randomized compiling re-signs the pieces;
when the per-round coefficients vary freely, no single instruction
reveals the angle.  We ask what a compiler that sees only the resulting
instruction stream must pay to merge the pieces into one rotation
per term.  For a contiguous layer of $m$ independent rotations on an
accuracy-$\epsilon$ grid, we prove that any compiler that is correct on
every such stream and reads it in forward passes must spend about
$m\log_2(1/\epsilon)$ bits.  They are counted as output committed while
reading the earlier rounds plus state carried into the last round on each
pass.  A one-pass compiler that emits one aggregated rotation per term
attains this to leading order, and so does a multipass compiler that
merges a different block of terms on each pass.  A one-pass stream
optimizer with a much smaller buffer must emit unmerged output early.  Memory and early output are not always
interchangeable.  Suppose $k$ of the $m$ terms, chosen at random, are
split over two rounds, and the compiler emits all their first-round
rotations before the second round, where they are applied.  That prefix
then needs on average at least $\log_2\binom{m}{k}$ extra bits if each record must
address its term, and a smaller amount, still linear in $k$ for fixed
$k/m<1$, if it need only implement the rotations.  A prefix buffered
until the next round is read need not pay extra.  Independently of the
bound, on a four-qubit benchmark that repeats one diagonal layer, under
one disclosed synthesis and surface-code model, Qiskit's highest preset
optimization level, given the pre-lowered circuit, needs 385--1{,}753
times the logical $T$ states of our compiler's output from the unlowered
circuit, which merges the repetitions but not the decomposition of each
controlled phase; the ratio grows with the number of repetitions.
TKET's PauliSimp, also given a pre-lowered circuit, merges across that
decomposition too and needs about half as many as ours.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Two standard steps of a fault-tolerant software stack can split rotation
angles.  A time-dependent schedule of a commuting Hamiltonian, or a block
of consecutive commuting layers with independent per-term
parameters~\cite{farhi2014qaoa,herrman2022multiangle,peruzzo2014vqe,cerezo2021variational,childs2021trotter},
applies an accumulated phase over several contiguous rounds, and when the
per-round coefficients vary freely no single round reveals the total angle
of a phase term.  Randomized compiling then dresses each round with random
Pauli gates, so that coherent errors average into stochastic Pauli
noise~\cite{wallman2016noise,hashim2021randomized}; for a $Z$-type
rotation, conjugation by a random Pauli flips the sign of its angle.  Both
steps are correct and useful, and together they leave the total angle
spread over many re-signed instructions (\cref{fig:intro-dispersal}a).
Standard QAOA and VQE layers, and generic Trotter steps, are separated by
non-commuting layers and do not by themselves form such blocks.

Whether the pieces are merged before gate synthesis matters for
fault-tolerant cost.  At accuracy $\epsilon$ a generic rotation costs about
$3\log_2(1/\epsilon)$ $T$ gates, roughly 30 at
$\epsilon=10^{-3}$~\cite{ross2016optimal}, every $T$ gate consumes a
distilled magic state~\cite{bravyi2005magic,litinski2019magic}, and a term
left in $r$ pieces is synthesized $r$ times.  Merging commuting rotations
is accordingly standard: phase folding and phase-polynomial
methods~\cite{amy2014tdepth,nam2018automated,amy2019cnot,amy2025relational,albarghouthi2026lineartime},
Pauli-gadget and ZX
rewriting~\cite{cowtan2020phase,kissinger2020reducing,vandewetering2025optimal},
and the Hamming-weight phasing of fault-tolerant resource
estimates~\cite{gidney2018halving,kivlichan2020improved} all exploit
commutation.  These methods assume an optimizer that sees, or can rebuild,
a global description of the layer.  Some compilation instead runs
downstream of the steps that dispersed it: a transpiler service that
receives a circuit already lowered to standard gates, for instance as
OpenQASM~\cite{cross2022openqasm}; a vendor pass that post-processes an
emitted instruction stream; or the classical control electronics, which
process the stream in real time and track Pauli gates in a
frame~\cite{riesebos2017pauli}.  We ask what a compiler in this position
must pay to merge the pieces: how much information it must hold between
rounds, and what it costs to commit output instead.

Our first result answers this question for a commuting layer of $m$
independent $Z$-type rotations whose angles lie on an
$\epsilon$-calibrated grid and arrive split over $r$ rounds
(\cref{fig:intro-dispersal}b).  Any compiler that is correct on every such
stream, makes at most $p$ forward scans, and fails with probability at
most $\delta<1/2$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier}
 \overline A_p+pS\;\geq\;(1-\delta)\,m\log_2K_\epsilon-h_2(\delta)
 -\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil
\end{equation}
(\cref{thm:w1-unified-frontier}), where $h_2$ is the binary entropy
function.  Here $K_\epsilon=\Theta(1/\epsilon)$ is
the number of angles of the grid, which is also the largest number of
rotations about a common axis that are pairwise $4\epsilon$-separated;
$K_\epsilon=785$ at $\epsilon=10^{-3}$.  $\overline A_p$ is the expected
number of output bits committed while the earlier rounds are scanned,
summed over passes, and $S$ caps the state carried across a crossing
between the earlier rounds and the last; of the $2p-1$ crossings that $p$
forward scans make, only the $p$ forward ones enter.  $S$ charges every
channel through which a compiler restarted at a crossing could
resume---RAM, caches, random seeds, any retained copy of the input, and
any intermediate representation---so rebuilding a global phase table is
allowed but paid for.  The final output alone also needs
$(1-\delta)m\log_2K_\epsilon-h_2(\delta)$ bits.  At $\delta=0$, a one-pass
compiler that keeps the running sums and emits one aggregated rotation per
term attains \cref{eq:frontier} to leading order, and so does a $p$-pass
compiler that merges a different block of terms on each pass, as long as
$p$ is small compared with $m/\log m$; a compiler given the layer in
aggregated form needs only $O(\log m\cdot\log(1/\epsilon))$ bits of state
(\cref{prop:w1-block-hybrid,prop:w1-semantic-upper}).

Per term the price is $\log_2K_\epsilon\approx9.6$ bits at
$\epsilon=10^{-3}$.  What \cref{eq:frontier} fixes is where those bits
must live, and this is where dispersal enters.  The inequality would hold
even if the last round were known in advance, but output written before
the last round arrives cannot yet contain the merged angles.  A one-pass
stream optimizer whose buffer is much smaller than $m\log_2K_\epsilon$
bits must therefore commit about that much unmerged output early, and a
deferred compiler, which commits nothing early, must carry all of it
across the crossings (\cref{thm:w1-one-pass-mask}).  The proof is a
standard multipass streaming
argument~\cite{alon1999space,guha2008tight,kushilevitz1997communication},
akin to streaming with a read-only input and a write-only
output~\cite{francois2014unidirectional}; what is specific to compilation
is the fooling family and the accounting.  When the grid-rounded
coefficients vary freely from round to round, time-dependent schedules and
layerwise-parameterized blocks can emit exactly such fooling streams, with
no added secret sharing.  Randomized compiling keeps them fooling streams
and does not create the dispersal; in frame-tracked form the pending Pauli
frame is itself $m$ bits that must cross or be committed, although a
compiler that folds each sign into its stored residue pays nothing beyond
\cref{eq:frontier}
(\cref{prop:physical-instantiation,prop:frame-cross-cut}).

\Cref{eq:frontier} charges committed output and carried state at par.  Our
second result shows when they are not interchangeable.  Take a two-round
stream in which only $k$ of the $m$ terms are dispersed, the $k$ drawn
uniformly at random so that one compiler and one output code must serve
every pattern, and suppose the compiler writes the first-round
records of all $k$ terms before the boundary and that this prefix is
applied there, before the next round is read---as in a control stack that
streams instructions to the device.  If each record must address its term,
the prefix needs on average at least $\log_2\binom{m}{k}$ bits beyond its
payload, about $h_2(k/m)/(k/m)$ bits per committed term, which a deferred
compiler does not pay (\cref{prop:commitment-toll}).  If the prefix need
only implement the first-round rotations, a smaller toll remains, still
linear in $k$: for fixed $\eta=k/m<1$ its rate is
$h_2(\eta(1-1/Q))/\eta-h_2(1/Q)$ bits per committed term, where $Q$ is the
number of grid angles: at $k=m/2$ this is $1.99$ against $2$ on the
$\epsilon=10^{-3}$ grid ($Q=K_\epsilon=785$) and $0.92$ against $2$ on
the coarse grid $Q=3$ (\cref{cor:unitary-toll}).  A compiler that writes the same content
irrevocably but applies it only after reading on pays nothing extra, so
the toll requires the prefix to be executed at the cut; irrevocability
alone does not create it.  It resembles the advice costs of online
algorithms~\cite{bockenhauer2014string,boyar2017online} and parallels
relaxed-irrevocability models of online
computation~\cite{boyar2022relaxing}.

A software model of the two-round stream with $Q=3$ and binary
aggregates, which we call the masked-share device (each binary aggregate
is hidden by its two shares until the second round), makes both results
concrete; its state at the cut is serialized and checked by restarting
from it.  As the dispersed fraction of a layer varies, the state of a
deferred compiler in this model grows within 5\% of $\log_23+1$ bits per
dispersed term at $m=32$, 64, and 128: its serializer stores each residue
and its frame bit separately, one bit above the proven floor of
$\log_23$ (\cref{fig:eta-sweep}).  Under a preregistered
protocol, an encoder that ranks the dispersal pattern attains
$\log_2\binom{m}{k}/k$ to within 0.06 bits per term over a 16-fold range
of $m$, and the buffered control pays zero (\cref{fig:commit-toll}).
These are constructive checks of a source-coding bound, not tests that
could have failed, and two preregistered attempts to detect the toll in
Qiskit and TKET output did not meet their design premise
(\cref{sec:commitment}).

For external toolchains we report behavior.  Tools that rebuild a global
representation are not defeated by dispersal: on matched flat
representations of a fixed-modulus target, including a masked
modular-share stream, PyZX and TKET's PauliSimp (in its recommended native
configuration) reach one rotation per active term.  The bounds price that
rebuild in state; they do not forbid it.  We also propagate rotation
counts through checked Clifford+$T$ synthesis and two surface-code
profiles under a common error budget (\cref{sec:ft-consequences}).  On a
four-qubit benchmark that repeats one diagonal layer, Qiskit's
optimization level~3, given the pre-lowered circuit, keeps about 12
rotations per repetition, while our reference compiler, a
representation-aware pass in the Unitary Compiler Collection (UCC), given
the unlowered circuit, keeps 22 in total.  Across 48 settings at each of two sizes, Qiskit's
output needs 385--1{,}753 times the logical $T$ states of ours, a ratio
that grows with the number of repetitions.  Our reference merges the
repetitions but keeps the three phase terms into which each controlled
phase decomposes (a full merge keeps 10); TKET's PauliSimp, given the
pre-lowered circuit, merges those too and needs 0.44--0.54 times our $T$
states.  The inputs differ between pipelines and the benchmark repeats a
layer rather than dispersing it, so these numbers follow from one
disclosed model, not from \cref{eq:frontier}, which counts bits rather
than gates.

The bounds concern compilers that see the stream after scheduling,
randomization, and lowering, and that read it in forward scans.  They need
the rounds of a layer to be contiguous, up to Clifford records that
permute the generators (\cref{lem:clifford-interleaving}), and the
coefficients to vary freely from round to round.  Non-commuting
interleavings, reverse scans, and uncharged random access to the input lie
outside them, and a compiler that sees the layer before scheduling and
lowering escapes them.  Read the other way, these limits are a design
rule: where the interface and latency budget allow, randomize and lower
late, and keep phase semantics until the coefficients have been merged.
We do not exhibit a production pipeline that emits such streams to a
memory-bounded downstream stage.

\section{Physical sources of dispersed streams}
\label{sec:physical-dispersed}

The streams of \cref{def:w1-dispersed-stream} are stated adversarially.
Here three standard mechanisms supply them: time-dependent scheduling
gives the additive decomposition, randomized compiling gives sign
symmetry, and the synthesis grid of \cref{cor:w1-epsilon-cyclic} gives the
modulus.  A \emph{scheduled stream} is the stream of
\cref{def:w1-dispersed-stream} with $K=Q$ and signed records
$\mathsf{Update}(t,j,a_j^{(t)},s_j^{(t)})$, $s_j^{(t)}\in\{\pm1\}$,
implementing $\prod_t\prod_jR_{P_j}(\Delta s_j^{(t)}a_j^{(t)})$, with
aggregate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scheduled-aggregate}
 x_j=\sum_{t=1}^r s_j^{(t)}a_j^{(t)}\bmod Q .
\end{equation}
\label{def:scheduled-stream}%
The residue $a_j^{(t)}$ is a round-$t$ physical coefficient rounded to the
grid and $s_j^{(t)}$ is a twirl variable ($s\equiv+1$ without twirling).
In \emph{folded} mode each sign is absorbed into the residue at emission,
as in compile-time randomized
compiling~\cite{wallman2016noise,hashim2021randomized}; in \emph{tracked}
mode the signs accumulate in a classical Pauli-frame register and are
settled downstream~\cite{knill2005realistically,riesebos2017pauli}.

\begin{lemma}[Clifford interleaving]
\label{lem:clifford-interleaving}
Let the rounds of a scheduled stream be separated by Clifford records that
are fixed across the family, and let $D_{t-1}$ be the accumulated Clifford
emitted before round $t$.  If each $D_{t-1}$ maps the generating set to
itself up to sign and permutation,
$D_{t-1}^\dagger P_jD_{t-1}=\varepsilon_j^{(t)}P_{\pi_t(j)}$, then moving
every Clifford record to the end yields a scheduled stream on the same
generators, followed by a fixed Clifford suffix, in which the round-$t$
record of generator $j$ contributes residue $a_j^{(t)}$ with sign
$\varepsilon_j^{(t)}s_j^{(t)}$ to coordinate $\pi_t(j)$.  The
normalization is computable from the prefix, and every statement about
scheduled streams applies to the normalized image.
\end{lemma}

The hypothesis holds for Pauli-frame layers, hence for the twirls of
randomized compiling, and for routing layers whose qubit relabeling
permutes the generators, such as the reflection of a chain.  It fails for
a Clifford that maps some $P_j$ to a product of generators, and it does
not cover the non-Clifford $R_X$ layers that separate the diagonal rounds
of a generic Trotter step.

\begin{proposition}[Physical instantiation]
\label{prop:physical-instantiation}
Each of the following mechanisms can emit scheduled streams.  Assume that
the grid-rounded coefficients may vary independently across the $r$
rounds.  Then every aggregate $x\in\mathbb Z_Q^m$ is realized by some
valid stream, and no record, and no prefix that ends before the last
round, determines it.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,label=(\alph*),topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Time-dependent Trotterization} of a commuting
$H(t)=\sum_jc_j(t)P_j$ with step $\tau$: round $t$ applies
$R_{P_j}(2c_j(t_t)\tau)$, and $s\equiv+1$.
\item \emph{Layerwise-parameterized phase layers}: a depth-$r$ block of
commuting layers with free coefficients $\theta_j^{(t)}$.
\item \emph{Randomized compiling} of (a) or (b), in folded or tracked
mode.  With independent uniform twirl bits and the canonical sharing of
\cref{prop:w1-stream-equivalence}, every signed residue is uniform on
$\mathbb Z_Q$ and independent of $x_j$.
\end{enumerate}
A constant schedule $a_j^{(t)}\equiv a_j$ is degenerate: its first round
determines the aggregate.
\end{proposition}

The coefficient-freedom hypothesis carries the weight of
\cref{prop:physical-instantiation}: the proposition records which streams
can occur, not that a given pipeline produces them.  Its two halves play
different roles.  The frontier needs only the earlier rounds to range over
$\mathbb Z_Q^m$ and would hold with a fixed last round.  The reading that
early output must be unmerged needs the last round to be free as well; if
the last-round residue of each term is confined to a few grid steps, as
for a schedule with small increments, a compiler can commit part of each
merged angle early.  The rounds must also be contiguous up to the Clifford
records of \cref{lem:clifford-interleaving}.  Contiguity arises
structurally in controlled-power ladders and phase estimation, whereas
coefficient freedom comes from time-dependent schedules and from
layerwise-parameterized blocks such as multi-angle
QAOA~\cite{herrman2022multiangle}.  A standard QAOA cost layer, in which
every round rescales one weight vector, is contiguous but not
coefficient-free, so the bounds do not constrain it.  For the edge
characters $Z_uZ_v$ of a connected graph the rank is $|V|-1$, and the
bounds apply with $m=|V|-1$ to a spanning tree.  Finally, the bounds
constrain compilers downstream of the schedule, the twirl, and the
lowering; a compiler that holds the $\lambda$-bit seed of a pseudorandom
twirl faces only $2^\lambda$ sign patterns, and the frame bound below
falls from $m$ to $\lambda$ bits.  The workloads of \cref{sec:workloads}
repeat identical terms within each commuting block or separate blocks by
non-commuting layers; they exercise aggregation, not this bound.

\begin{proposition}[Frame as cross-cut state]
\label{prop:frame-cross-cut}
Let a compiler be correct within $\epsilon<\sin(\pi/(2Q))$ on every
twirled scheduled stream.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em,label=(\roman*),topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item In either mode, sign normalization maps the stream onto a valid
stream of \cref{def:w1-dispersed-stream}, so
\cref{thm:w1-unified-frontier,thm:w1-one-pass-mask} apply to the physical
stream under their own hypotheses; with the tuned modulus $Q_\epsilon$,
$0<\epsilon\leq1/8$, \cref{thm:w1-unified-frontier} gives
\cref{eq:frontier}.
\item The pending frame classes alone force the following.  A
deterministic, zero-error, one-pass compiler whose output committed before
an inter-round cut $c$ has length $A_{\mathrm{pre}}$ satisfies, in the
worst case, $A_{\mathrm{pre}}+B_{\mathrm{cut}}(c)\geq m$.  A randomized
compiler correct with probability at least $1-\delta$, $\delta<1/2$,
making at most $p$ forward passes, has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame-lower-multipass}
 \overline A_p+\overline\Sigma^{\to}_p
 \geq(1-\delta)m-h_2(\delta)-\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil .
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The frame register is live classical restart state and is charged in the
snapshot.  The frame does not add to \cref{eq:frontier}, because the
target depends on the signs only through \cref{eq:scheduled-aggregate} and
a deferred compiler, one whose committed prefix is only a header, may fold
each pending sign into the residue it stores.

Real layers need not be dispersed in every term.  For a coupled family,
let each generator independently with probability $\eta$ arrive in
dispersed twirled form and otherwise in aggregate-exposed form, as a
single round-two record that carries its aggregate.  We call $\eta$ the \emph{dispersal fraction}; it
is not the twirl strength, and the bound below does not use the twirl.

\begin{proposition}[Dispersal--memory bound]
\label{prop:eta-pareto}
Let $Q$, $K$, $\epsilon$, $\delta$, and $p$ be as in
\cref{thm:w1-unified-frontier}, and let the compiler be correct within
$\epsilon$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ on every valid stream for
every mask.  With the dispersal mask $T$ drawn as above and $\overline A_p(T)$, $S(T)$ the
worst-input quantities over streams with mask $T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-pareto}
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_T\bigl[\overline A_p(T)+pS(T)\bigr]\\
 &\quad\geq(1-\delta)\,\eta m\log_2K-h_2(\delta)-\lceil\log_2(p+1)\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a deterministic one-pass zero-error compiler and $K\in\{Q-1,Q\}$,
$\mathbb E_T[A_1(T)+B_{\mathrm{cut}}(T)]\geq\eta m\log_2Q$.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{fig:eta-sweep} executes \cref{eq:eta-pareto} on the masked-share
device of \cref{sec:introduction}, our executable implementation of the
coupled family above with $Q=3$, $K=2$, $r=2$, and tracked signs.
The two payment channels of the bound appear as the two panels, and the
exchange between them is linear in $\eta$ with slope proportional to $m$.
The panels also show an asymmetry that the frontier does not predict: per
dispersed generator the state channel costs about $2.6$ bits and the
committed channel about $16$, a ratio near $6{:}1$ between two currencies
that the frontier charges at par.  The next section isolates the part of
that gap that is not an artifact of the serializer.

\section{Executed commitment}
\label{sec:commitment}

Isolating that part needs one hypothesis beyond the charged model, and the
hypothesis is substantive: without it the gap is zero.

\begin{definition}[Executed and buffered commitment]
\label{def:self-identifying}
Consider a two-round stream with dispersal mask $T\subseteq[m]$: each
coordinate in $T$ has one addressed signed record in each round (kept even
when its residue is zero), and each coordinate outside $T$ has one
aggregate-exposed record, of a distinguishable type, in round two.
Suppose the compiler has irrevocably written the round-one content
$W:=(a^{(1)}_j,s^{(1)}_j)_{j\in T}$ before the inter-round cut, as the
prefix $O_{\mathrm{pre}}$ of length $A_{\mathrm{pre}}:=|O_{\mathrm{pre}}|$
($A_1$ of \cref{def:w1-pass-accounting} for one pass).  The
commitment is \emph{executed} if the prefix is applied at the cut, before
any round-two record is parsed.  It is executed \emph{at record level} if
it dispatches one addressed record per dispersed generator, as a control
stack that forwards records to the device does, so that it determines
$(T,W)$ on its own; this is a modelling assumption, not a consequence of
applying a unitary.  It is executed \emph{at unitary level} if it is only
required, on its own and for every seed, to implement within $\epsilon$
the round-one action $\prod_{j\in T}R_{P_j}(\Delta s^{(1)}_ja^{(1)}_j)$,
which fixes $y\in\mathbb Z_Q^m$ with $y_j=s^{(1)}_ja^{(1)}_j$ on $T$ and
$y_j=0$ off $T$, but neither $T$ nor $W$.  The commitment is
\emph{buffered} if the prefix is written once but applied only after later
records have been read.  A compiler is \emph{mask-uniform} if one
algorithm is correct on every mask, its committed prefixes over all masks
form one prefix-free set, and the action of a prefix does not depend on
$T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Commitment toll]
\label{prop:commitment-toll}
Fix $Q\geq3$, $\epsilon<\sin(\pi/(2Q))$, and $1\leq k\leq m$.  Let
$\mathcal A$ be mask-uniform and correct within $\epsilon$ on every stream
of \cref{def:self-identifying}.  Draw $T$ uniformly among the
$\binom{m}{k}$ subsets of size $k$ and $W$ uniformly on
$\mathbb Z_Q^k\times\{\pm1\}^k$, independently.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.6em,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item If $\mathcal A$ commits in record-level executed form, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commitment-toll}
 \mathbb E_{T,W}\bigl[A_{\mathrm{pre}}\bigr]
 \;\geq\;\underbrace{k\log_2(2Q)}_{\text{payload}}
 \;+\;\underbrace{\log_2\tbinom{m}{k}}_{\text{toll}},
\end{equation}
whereas a deferred compiler attains $A_{\mathrm{pre}}=0$ with crossing
state $S\leq k\log_2(2Q)+O(\log m)$.  Among prefixes that determine
$(T,W)$, ranking $T$ among the $\binom mk$ masks attains
\cref{eq:commitment-toll} up to $O(\log m)$.
\item For $k=\Theta(m)$ with $k\leq m-\Omega(m)$, the hypothesis in (i)
cannot be removed: a compiler that writes $W$ irrevocably in positional
order and recovers $T$ from the round-two records is admissible, commits
in buffered form, and has
$\mathbb E[A_{\mathrm{pre}}]\leq k\log_2(2Q)+O(\log m)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By \cref{def:self-identifying} a record-level executed prefix
determines $(T,W)$, so $H(O_{\mathrm{pre}})\geq H(T,W)$.  By mask
uniformity the committed prefixes form one prefix-free code over the
joint source $(T,W)$, and the expected length of a prefix-free code is at
least the entropy of its source; hence
$\mathbb E[A_{\mathrm{pre}}]=\mathbb E|O_{\mathrm{pre}}|\geq H(T,W)$.
By construction, $T$ and $W$ are independent and uniform, so
$H(T,W)=\log_2\binom{m}{k}+k\log_2(2Q)$, which is
\eqref{eq:commitment-toll}, and a maximum dominates a mean.  For the
deferred compiler the round-major order is public, so its snapshot holds
the $k$ residues and signs in positional order while the round-two record
types distinguish dispersed updates from aggregate-exposed coordinates
and thereby disclose the mask at no charge;
$W$ is held as one packed integer of $\lceil k\log_2(2Q)\rceil$ bits in
the store field, and the $O(\log m)$ term is the header.
Ranking $T$ among the $\binom mk$ masks gives a prefix of length
$\lceil\log_2\binom mk\rceil+\lceil k\log_2(2Q)\rceil+O(\log m)$ that
determines $(T,W)$, which shows the bound is tight up to the header in that
class.

(ii) The mask-deferred compiler never rewrites an emitted record, so it
meets the write-once rule of \cref{sec:w1-dispersed}, and it is correct because the round-two
records supply $T$ before the final output is written.  Its prefix
holds $W$ in positional order at $k\log_2(2Q)$ bits plus a header, and
reveals no function of $T$ beyond the value of $k$ already implied by
its length, so no $\log_2\binom{m}{k}$ term arises.  There is no
conflict with (i): this prefix does not determine $(T,W)$, hence is not
executable at the cut, and the compiler has bought its exemption by
giving up the ability to apply what it has written.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The toll at unitary level]
\label{cor:unitary-toll}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:commitment-toll}, let $\mathcal A$
commit in unitary-level executed form, and let
$\nu\sim\mathrm{Bin}(k,1-1/Q)$ count the dispersed coordinates with
nonzero signed residue.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-toll}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{T,W}\bigl[A_{\mathrm{pre}}\bigr]
 &\geq H(y)\\
 &=\log_2\tbinom{m}{k}+k\log_2Q
 -\mathbb E\Bigl[\log_2\tbinom{m-\nu}{k-\nu}\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
while a deferred compiler that folds each sign into its stored residue
attains $A_{\mathrm{pre}}=0$ with $S\leq k\log_2Q+O(\log m)$.  For fixed
$\eta=k/m$ and $m\to\infty$, the toll per committed generator tends to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unitary-toll-rate}
 \frac{h_2\bigl(\eta(1-1/Q)\bigr)}{\eta}-h_2(1/Q),
\end{equation}
and this rate is attained by an executed prefix that writes $\nu$, the
rank of $\operatorname{supp}y$, and the nonzero values of $y$ packed in
base $Q-1$.  The rate
is positive for $0<\eta<1$, vanishes at $\eta=1$, and increases with $Q$
to the record-level rate $h_2(\eta)/\eta$; at $\eta=\tfrac12$ it is
$0.918$ bits for $Q=3$ and $1.99$ bits for $Q=785$, against $2$ at
record level.
\end{corollary}

The proof of \cref{cor:unitary-toll} is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.  The toll is a property of executed
commitment, not of commitment as such: a deferred or buffered compiler
learns the mask from the round-two records.  Where it is paid, the
record-level toll is at most $h_2(\eta)/\eta$ bits per generator, about
$2$ at $\eta=\tfrac12$ and $0$ at $\eta=1$, and does not grow with $m$.
For a single mask known in advance there is nothing to pay; mask
uniformity is what makes the dispersal pattern a source.  The two
degeneracies of the unitary---a zero signed residue acts as the identity,
and $(s,a)$ and $(-s,-a)$ give the same rotation---are why the
unitary-level toll is smaller.  It is proved but not measured.

\Cref{fig:commit-toll} instantiates both clauses of
\cref{prop:commitment-toll} on the masked-share device under a protocol
frozen before the run (\path{PaperDraft/research/E8_preregistration.md}).
It compares the deferred snapshot, the address-per-record serializer of
\cref{fig:eta-sweep}, and an executed prefix that ranks the mask, at
$m\in\{128,512,2048\}$ and up to $m=32768$ for two encoders.  The
measured excess matches $\log_2\binom{m}{k}/k$ within $0.06$ bits over
the preregistered analysis cells and is flat in $m$ ($2.11/1.99/1.97$
bits at $\eta=\tfrac12$).  The control writes the same payload into the
same write-once output and rewrites nothing, so what separates it from the
executed encoder is not irreversibility but whether the prefix can be
applied before the suffix is read.  The $6{:}1$ ratio of
\cref{fig:eta-sweep} is mostly codec slack: under the ranking encoder,
which writes only what an executed prefix must determine, the ratio is at
most $2.64$, and only the slack grows with
address width.

Two preregistered attempts to carry the toll onto third-party compilers
failed, and we report them as failures.  The first, which asked whether
toolchains spend committed bytes on addresses, placed every rotation on
low-index qubits and so never varied the address width.  The second
compared circuits that
differ only in whether the dispersed subset is an arithmetic progression
or a random $k$-subset, emitted through Qiskit and TKET and compared by
compressed size; the excess reached $86\%$ of $\log_2\binom{m}{k}$ on
TKET but peaked at the wrong $k$, and was within seed noise on Qiskit.  A
general-purpose compressor may be too coarse an entropy estimator, and
neither tool emits a round-major committed stream, so the design premise
was not met.  Whether the toll is measurable at a third-party interface
remains open; details are in the Supplemental Material~\cite{supplement}.

\label{sec:compiler}
\label{sec:validation}
\label{sec:external-baselines}

We now leave the stream model and report how our reference compiler and
external toolchains handle repeated and dispersed layers.  The reference
compiler is a representation-aware pass in a frozen snapshot of the
Unitary Compiler Collection.  It recognizes a fixed
grammar of high-level forms---explicit Pauli rotations and phase gadgets,
local phase-ladder and Fourier spans, mirrored composite blocks, and
repeated semantic nodes---keys the terms of each accepted commuting span by
generator, adds their coefficients, and emits at most one record per
generator, so the output has $O(m)$ phase records independently of the
number of repetitions.  It does not implement a general symplectic
extractor and claims no maximal commuting region.  Every selected rewrite
must pass an independent equivalence certificate: a dense unitary check for
$n\leq6$, or a sound but incomplete exact symbolic checker that propagates
a Clifford tableau through the circuit, extracts the rotation axes, and
compares canonical records~\cite{supplement}; failure returns the
unchanged candidate.  On the supported exact domain,
\CertificateMutationCases\ adversarial mutations produce
\CertificateFalseAccepts\ false accepts, and \CertificateCrosscheckCases\
dense--symbolic cross-checks show no disagreement.  Only certified
\texttt{completed\_valid} rows enter quality means; unsupported, invalid,
and inconclusive outcomes are reported as status.

The external toolchain results are configured-pipeline diagnostics; they
report tool behavior and neither prove nor test a lower bound.  A matched
campaign of \MatchedCells\ cells evaluates five compilers on nine
representations of the same target---semantic aggregate, contiguous,
round-robin, and randomly ordered flat streams, a masked modular-share
stream, a locally folded stream, and QASM, TKET, and ZX bridge
records---with a common target basis, error budget, timeout, memory cap,
and certificate policy; \MatchedCompleted\ cells are
\texttt{completed\_valid} and \MatchedPredicateErrors\ are retained as
\texttt{predicate\_error}.  The matched target uses a fixed modulus and
angles that do not wrap, not the $\epsilon$-calibrated grid, and an active
term is one with nonzero aggregate.  A separate \ExternalCells-cell
campaign freezes recommended-native and unified-basis configurations of
Qiskit, TKET PauliSimp, PyZX \texttt{full\_reduce}, and an exact
phase-table baseline of the authors, distinct from the reference compiler
(\ExternalCompleted\ completed, \ExternalPredicateErrors\ predicate
errors).  TKET PauliSimp, in its recommended native configuration, and
PyZX recover one rotation per active term on the flat representations, so
we classify them as semantic-capable: starting from the lowered stream,
they rebuild a global intermediate representation in which the terms can
be merged, and the bounds charge that representation as state.

On a fixed-width benchmark that repeats one four-qubit diagonal
layer---four single-qubit $Z$ rotations and six controlled phases on all
pairs---between Hadamard layers (\cref{fig:fourier-separation}), our
semantic path returns a constant 42 gates and rebased TKET PauliSimp 52--73
gates over five sizes from 4k to 100k requested input gates, while Qiskit's
optimization level~3 and staq's rotation folding grow linearly with the
input.  Disabling the Fourier-layer recognition makes our own compiler
grow linearly as well, so the constant output comes from semantic lifting,
not from pass order.  TKET's GuidedPauliSimp acts only inside
\texttt{CircBox} regions and returns these flat inputs unchanged in size.
The full matrices, the packing-family and width scans, and a
real-instance anti-regression panel are in the Supplemental
Material~\cite{supplement}.

A preregistered scan (\path{PaperDraft/research/E7_preregistration.md})
ran TKET PauliSimp on four-round dispersed streams with modulus $64$ on
chains of $n\in\{8,\ldots,128\}$ qubits ($n-1$ terms).  It reduced the
non-Clifford rotations to one per active term in every cell ($508$
dispersed rotations to $127$ at $n=128$), although its total output was not
compact, and its process-memory increment grew with log--log slope
$1.70$ over the range selected by the preregistered noise-floor rule,
outside the predicted interval $[0.7,1.5]$; the prediction was therefore
rejected.  A process-memory increment is not $B_{\mathrm{cut}}$, the scan
has no arm on the aggregated layer, and its input text grows with $m$, so
it describes PauliSimp's behavior rather than testing the frontier.

\section{Fault-tolerant resource consequences}
\label{sec:ft-consequences}

The bounds count bits; they do not convert into gates or magic states.  We
therefore propagate the measured outputs of three pipelines through
Clifford+$T$ synthesis and surface-code scheduling under one disclosed,
versioned model, on the four-qubit repeated-layer benchmark at 4k and 20k
requested input gates (399 and 1{,}999 repetitions of the layer).  We call
a pipeline semantic-first if the aggregate structure is available before
lowering and materialize-first if recovery starts from the lowered
stream.  The materialize-first pipeline is Qiskit's
optimization level~3 (trivial layout, no routing) applied after lowering
to $\{cx,rx,ry,rz,h\}$; TKET PauliSimp receives the same circuit lowered to
a $cx$--$R_z$--$h$ bridge; and the semantic-first reference receives the

$0.10\epsilon_{\rm total}$ for the certified algorithmic stage,
$0.45\epsilon_{\rm total}$ for synthesis, and $0.45\epsilon_{\rm total}$

deterministic build of staq

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph*{Rotation merging.}
Commuting-rotation aggregation is a standard optimization
target~\cite{amy2014tdepth,nam2018automated,cowtan2020phase,sivarajah2021tket,duncan2020graph,kissinger2020reducing,kissinger2020pyzx}.
Phase-polynomial methods make the generator--coefficient structure
explicit and re-synthesize the parity
network~\cite{amy2019cnot,beaudrap2020techniques,meijer2023architecture},
and ZX methods keep a global normal form in which phases fuse across
syntactic boundaries~\cite{vandewetering2020zx,kissinger2022simulating}.
These are upper-bound constructions; our bounds go the other way and charge
the global representation such tools build as compiler state.

\paragraph*{Synthesis and resource estimation.}
Generic $Z$-rotations cost $\Theta(\log(1/\epsilon))$ Clifford+$T$
gates~\cite{dawson2006solovay,kliuchnikov2013fast,ross2016optimal,selinger2015efficient,gheorghiu2022tcount}.
End-to-end estimates propagate non-Clifford counts through distillation
and layout~\cite{bravyi2005magic,gidney2021factor,beverland2022assessing,babbush2021focus};
our QRE campaign is an instance of this methodology under a disclosed
model, not a new estimator.

\paragraph*{Intermediate representations and routing.}
Quantum software stacks preserve information across lowering in
intermediate
representations~\cite{steiger2018projectq,javadi2024qiskit,sivarajah2021tket,watkins2024surfacecode,peduri2022qssa,mccaskey2021mlir};
our model charges what such representations retain as $B_{\mathrm{IR}}$.
Routing and platform compilers reshape the stream a downstream stage
sees~\cite{cowtan2019routing,li2019sabre,martiel2022architecture,kreppel2023shuttling,tan2024dpqa};
their SWAP layers normalize away only under the hypothesis of
\cref{lem:clifford-interleaving}.

\paragraph*{Noise tailoring.}
Randomized compiling and Pauli
twirling~\cite{wallman2016noise,hashim2021randomized,knill2005realistically}
act on the dispersed streams without creating them, and error
mitigation~\cite{cai2023mitigation,vandenberg2023probabilistic} shares the
relevant feature: the executed circuit is a randomized ensemble whose
members do not display the source coefficients.

\paragraph*{Lower-bound methodology.}
The proofs import fooling-set and communication
arguments~\cite{kushilevitz1997communication,sipser2012introduction} and
the space--passes tradition of streaming
computation~\cite{munro1980selection,alon1999space,guha2008tight,francois2014unidirectional}.
The commitment toll is related to advice
complexity~\cite{bockenhauer2014string,boyar2017online} and to
relaxed irrevocability~\cite{boyar2022relaxing}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results have different scopes.  \Cref{thm:w1-unified-frontier} covers
$r$-round shares, randomized failure, and $p=p(m)$ forward scans re-delivered
by the source, and is attained to leading order for $p$ small compared
with $m/\log m$; \cref{thm:w1-one-pass-mask} gives the sharper integer form
for deterministic one-pass compilers.  Reverse scans, uncharged random
access to the input, and a different output code are outside both.  The
commitment toll is the most narrowly scoped result: it needs the prefix to
be executed at the cut, it is an ensemble statement about one mask-uniform
compiler, and only its record-level form is measured, on our own software
model.
The physical reading needs contiguous rounds, freely varying coefficients,
and a compiler downstream of the schedule and the twirl; we have not
exhibited a production pipeline that emits such streams to a bounded
downstream stage, and that is the main open question on the applied side.
The resource campaign uses actual synthesis and executable surface-code
calculations, but under one phenomenological model family and with
asymmetric inputs, and its benchmark repeats rather than disperses a
layer; the workloads are certified at six qubits, and their size axis
repeats structure rather than widening it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A compiler that receives a commuting phase layer whose rounds are
contiguous, up to generator-permuting Clifford records, and whose
per-round coefficients vary freely, possibly re-signed by randomized
compiling, and that reads it in forward passes and is correct with
probability at least $1-\delta$ on every such stream, must hold or have
committed $(1-\delta)m\log_2K_\epsilon-h_2(\delta)$ bits, about
$\log_2(1/\epsilon)$ per independent term.  At $\delta=0$ a one-pass
compiler that keeps the running sums, or a $p$-pass compiler that merges
one block of terms per pass, meets that price to leading order.  The two
channels part ways when the committed first-round rotations are applied
at the cut: the prefix must then pay for the dispersal pattern, the full
entropy if it addresses its targets and a smaller but linear amount if it
only implements them, while buffered output pays nothing.  Under one
disclosed synthesis and surface-code model, Qiskit's highest preset
optimization level, given the pre-lowered circuit, needs 385--1{,}753
times the logical $T$ states of our compiler's output from the unlowered
circuit, a ratio that grows with the number of repetitions, while TKET's
PauliSimp, which rebuilds the phase structure, needs about half as many as
ours; the inputs differ, so these ratios measure what is at stake when
repetitions reach synthesis unmerged.  The cost of recovering merged
rotations from a dispersed stream is thus a quantity that a software stack
can budget for: where the interface allows, randomize and lower late, and
keep phase semantics until the coefficients have been merged.

Two passages from the classical Chinese canon shaped parts of this work.
Their relevance is direct, and the acknowledgements provide a fitting
context in which to quote them.

The first is from the \emph{Xici} commentary to the \emph{Yijing}: ``What
is above form is called the Way; what is below form is called the vessel;
to transform and tailor it is called change; to carry it forward and put it
into practice is called continuity.''  What is useful here is not an
analogy but a set of distinctions.  The passage names four things
separately, and ranks none of them above another: a description that
carries structure, a description that carries only instructions, the act of
lowering the one into the other, and the act of execution.  A compiler is
precisely the engine that performs the third and hands off the fourth; and
the vocabulary of compilation itself---semantic level, lowering,
commitment---reproduces the same fourfold division without, so far as we
know, having borrowed it.  The lesson we draw from the passage is that
the level at which a computation is described is not a notational
convenience to be optimized away, but a variable that can be charged for.
This paper charges it, in bits.

The second is Chapter 64 of the \emph{Laozi}: ``What is at rest is easy to
hold; what has not yet shown a sign is easy to plan for; what is brittle is
easy to shatter; what is minute is easy to scatter.  Act on it before it
comes to be; order it before it falls into disorder.''  Every clause here
prices an action by the state of the object acted upon rather than by the
action itself---which is exactly this paper's claim about representation:
the same aggregation is cheap or expensive depending only on the form in
which the layer arrives.  The verb of the first clause, \emph{to hold}, is
the operation charged here as compiler state.  The chapter further
separates planning early, which it recommends, from grasping early, warning
that ``one who grasps it loses it''; that separation is,
here, the difference between a compiler that defers and one that commits,
and the price of the difference is the commitment toll.  And the rule on
which the chapter closes is the design rule of our abstract, read from the
other side.

Neither passage contains a theorem, and neither anticipates one; no result
reported here depends on either.  A reader who finds these readings
strained may discard them entirely and judge the paper by whatever in it
proves useful.  They are recorded because the question did come from there
and because, for a framing as for a datum, a reader may rightly ask where it
came from.  Translations are J.Y.'s own.
